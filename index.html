The gap

Global heating isn't shared out evenly, and neither is who pays for it. Oxfam found that the wealthiest tenth of humanity produces about half of all carbon emissions, while the poorest half — three and a half billion people — produces roughly a tenth. Those same 3.5 billion live overwhelmingly in the countries most exposed to what the emissions cause.

So the floods, fires, droughts, and displacement land first and hardest on Indigenous nations, Black and brown communities, island nations, and the Global South: the people who did the least to cause this, and who have the fewest resources to recover. That distance between who broke it and who's paying for it is the injustice sitting inside the climate crisis. Closing it is the actual work — not just lowering a number, but repairing a relationship.

Calling in, not calling out

Youth climate organizer Xiye Bastida writes about calling people into the climate movement instead of calling them out of it — building something wide enough for millions of imperfect, still-learning people, instead of a clean room reserved for a righteous few.

That's the spirit here. If you've felt too behind, too implicated, too unqualified, or too tired to start — there is no entrance exam. You're already in. Bring what you have.

Art goes first

Change rarely starts in a courtroom or a spreadsheet. It starts in a song, a mural, a meme forwarded between friends, a poem read out loud. Artist and cultural strategist Favianna Rodriguez argues that culture moves before policy does — we can't legislate what we haven't first imagined together.

This page is a small piece of that work. Not a lecture — a poster. Not a report — a wall you're meant to stop in front of.

The call

Not five things. One thing, and you can do it before you close this tab.

Do this now — 60 seconds

Call one person in.

Think of someone in your life who isn't in yet — not a denier, just someone who cares and hasn't found a way in. Text them. Say a version of this:

"I've been reading about climate justice — not just the weather part, the part about who's actually paying for it. I don't totally know what to do yet either. Want to figure it out with me?"

That's it. You're not recruiting, converting, or lecturing. You're widening the door by exactly one person — and asking them to widen it again.

If you want to go further

  • Find out whose land you live on, and who's already organizing there — join first, found second.
  • Back Indigenous-led and frontline-led groups with money, time, or a real skill — like the Indigenous Environmental Network or NDN Collective.
  • Make something — a post, a print, a playlist, a conversation — that makes this feel true, not just correct.
  • Show up again next week. This isn't a one-time signature. It's a practice.

The movement doesn't need you perfect.
It needs you present.
